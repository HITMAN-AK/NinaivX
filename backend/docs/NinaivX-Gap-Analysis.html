NinaivX — Literature Gap Analysis

What prior work lacks, the gaps it reveals, and the requirements those gaps generate · Ashwin Kumar Ramesh Kumar · MSc AI, University of Sheffield

This document distils the Literature Survey into a gap analysis and links it directly to the Requirements Specification. Each gap carries an ID (G1–G9) used identically in the Requirements (MoSCoW) document, and each row shows the requirements that gap generated. Reference numbers [n] match the Literature Survey. Where a commercial system does not publicly document a safeguard, this is stated as “not documented” rather than asserted as absent.

HereAfter AI An interactive “life story” answering in the deceased's own recorded voice. Replays pre-recorded, consented clips only — cannot answer questions it was never asked. G1 Generative, real-time conversation that responds to novel questions, with memory across sessions. M5, M6, M9 [16]
Project December A GPT-3 “griefbot”; free-form chat built from a person's messages. Text-only; unconstrained model can fabricate memories; a single anecdotal case with no evaluation. G2, G3, G8 Full voice loop; air-gapped and grounded to curb fabrication; safety design empirically evaluated. M7–M9, M10, M13, M15 [17]
Replika A popular AI companion; text chat; emotional relationship. Text-heavy; engagement-driven design linked to emotional dependence and harm; not designed for grief. G2, G5 Voice-first interaction; oriented to comfort and closure, with no engagement-maximising mechanics. M7–M9, S10 [12–15]
XiaoIce An empathetic social chatbot engineered for long-term engagement. Text-based; optimised to maximise engagement; no bereavement safeguards. G2, G5 Voice; closure-oriented design rather than engagement maximisation. M7–M9, S10 [11]
Microsoft patent A method to build a chatbot of a specific person (incl. deceased) from their data. A design claim, not a deployed or reproducible system; consent and privacy for the deceased unresolved. G6, G7 A working, openly documented implementation with authentication and strict per-user data isolation. M1, M11, M14 [18]
You, Only Virtual (YOV) Commercial voice griefbot: phone-call interface, voice cloning, memory, generative conversation. Frames grief as “a problem to be solved”; no documented safety or transparency; closed/proprietary; unevaluated; deceased-only. G3, G4, G5, G7, G8, G9 Air-gap safety; AI disclosure; closure-oriented stance; open architecture; empirical evaluation; adds a companion mode. M10, M14, M15, S9, S10, M3, S1 [26–27]
Unconstrained LLMs (e.g. GPT-3) Highly fluent, human-like text generation. Prone to “hallucination” — inventing facts or opinions the real person never had. G3 Deceased persona air-gapped from the internet (code-level) and grounded in stored personality + memory. M4, M10, M13 [3][19]
